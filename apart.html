THE LEGACY OF ABSENCE

Sometimes the memory is what never happened.

A missing bedtime story. A question that was never asked. A parent who was physically present but emotionally distant. A child who grows up knowing the dates and photographs, but not what their parent thought, felt or wanted them to know.

MIA cannot repair a relationship, decide why somebody was absent or replace therapy, family support or professional processes. What it can do is preserve genuine words, voice, photographs and context so important human meaning is not lost simply because people are apart.

The aim is simple: where a person has something real to say, preserve it in their own words while there is still time.

Reading that shaped this thinking

The Absent Father Effect on Daughters

Susan E. Schwartz
Father Desire, Father Wounds

The book explores the experience of daughters whose fathers were physically absent, emotionally unavailable or difficult to reach. One idea is especially relevant to MIA: presence is not only about being in the same room. Being known, heard and emotionally recognised matters too.

WHAT MIA CAN PRESERVE

Give the future something more than silence.

For families separated by work, illness, geography, relationship breakdown or other circumstances, MIA is designed to keep authentic material that may matter later.

Your real voice

Messages, stories and ordinary conversations recorded in your own voice, not recreated as if you said something you never did.

What you wanted them to know

Your own words about love, pride, memories, mistakes, hopes and the things that can be difficult to say face to face.

The ordinary moments

Not only major milestones. The jokes, routines, places and small details that make a relationship human.

Context without rewriting history

What you remember can sit alongside dates, photographs and source material without turning one person's recollection into unquestioned fact.

Messages for later

Where appropriate, material can carry clear instructions about who it is for and when you intended it to be considered.

A record of care

When distance makes everyday presence impossible, a genuine record can still show that somebody thought, remembered and cared.
