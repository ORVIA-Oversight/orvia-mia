WHY MIA

THIS STARTED WITH MIA.

A real idea born from separation, time, love and the belief that important human memories should not disappear.

A memory product built from a human problem.

MIA began with one dad wanting his daughter to know that years apart never meant years forgotten. The purpose is not to rewrite history, attack another person or ask a child to take sides. It is simply to preserve what is honestly yours to tell.

Birthdays pass. Christmas passes. Ordinary days pass. Photographs become older. There are jokes, memories, family stories, places, songs and pieces of your own history you may want somebody to know.

The idea grew.

Families spend time apart for many reasons: work, military deployment, offshore and maritime work, hospital stays, living in different countries, changing family circumstances, serious illness and death. Separation should not automatically erase human connection.

Founding principle: Source before synthesis. If somebody did not say it, MIA should not pretend they did.

What MIA is trying to protect.

Memory

Preserve the original moment, voice, photograph or story.

Identity

Keep the people, relationships and context around a memory visible.

Authenticity

Be clear about what is original, edited, generated, inferred or unknown.

The test for every future feature.

Does this preserve something human, or is the technology trying to replace the human?

If it replaces the human, MIA should stop. If it preserves human truth carefully and transparently, it may continue.
