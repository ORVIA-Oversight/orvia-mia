Stories & voice

Sometimes their voice is the memory.

A photograph can preserve a face. A voice recording preserves the laugh, accent, pauses and way somebody told a story. MIA keeps the original recording alongside anything created from it.

“Tell me about the house where you grew up.”One question can unlock a story nobody else knows.
Record a story with us

Their words. Not ours.

If they said it, preserve it. If they did not, say so.

MIA's future interactive experiences are being designed around genuine source material — not invented memories or a system guessing what somebody “would have said”.

For Later

Some words belong to another day.

Record a message, story or wish now and keep clear instructions beside it: who it is for, what you intended, and when you would want it considered for release.

Important: the current prototype can record release instructions. Production release, identity verification and post-death workflows are still being developed and legally reviewed. AI will not decide whether somebody has died, is “ready”, or should receive sensitive material.

The founder behind MIA

Built inside ORVIA’s human-first approach.

John McGill founded ORVIA Oversight around a simple idea: paperwork is rarely the whole story. His background includes senior operational responsibility across independent ambulance services, regulated care, local government and overseas security, alongside ongoing professional development in safeguarding, health and social care leadership and strategic management.

MIA takes that same discipline into something more personal: preserving the real words, photographs, voices and context that make up a person’s story — without manufacturing what was never there.
