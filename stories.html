Stories & voice

Margaret’s story.

A photograph can show a family standing by the sea. It cannot tell you who hated the wind, who always bought the chips, which joke made everyone laugh, or why that ordinary Sunday became one of the memories the family kept talking about.

What MIA keeps

Not just what happened. What it meant.

This example shows the kind of context MIA can preserve around a memory. The people and story below are illustrative.

01 · FAMILY

Family first.

Margaret’s stories were rarely about herself alone. They were about the people around her and the small routines that made a family feel like home.

02 · PLACE

Seaside Sundays.

Scarborough was more than a location. It was windbreaks, paper-wrapped chips, the same café and the walk everyone complained about but never stopped taking.

03 · OBJECTS

A note for everything.

Lunchboxes, birthday cards and suitcases all seemed to contain a handwritten note. The words changed. The reassurance behind them did not.

04 · VOICE

The way she said it.

The sentence matters. So does the laugh before it, the pause, the accent and the way a person tells the story in their own words.

“The little things are often the things everyone remembers.”

Their words. Not ours.

Voice makes a memory human.

Where an original recording exists, MIA can keep it with the transcript and the story around it. A transcript helps people find and understand a memory; it does not replace the source recording.

Where no recording exists, MIA does not invent one and present it as the person’s real voice.

One memory object

Everything that belongs together.

MIA is designed to keep the human story and its provenance together without cluttering the experience.

The original

The photograph, voice note, video, document or source file remains distinguishable from edited versions.

The people

Who was there, how people are connected and whose recollection is being recorded.

The place

The location as supplied by the family, without silently inventing coordinates or context.

The story

What happened, why it mattered and the details a camera could never capture on its own.

The confidence

Known, verified, supported, inferred or unknown can remain distinct rather than being flattened into certainty.

The future

Messages for later can carry explicit human-controlled release instructions alongside the original material.
